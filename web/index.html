Public Overview

Physical models that accelerate AI's world experience and its training.

Meadow is a physics-grounded learning approach. Engineers provide a typed specification of the robot and the goal; the system produces a deployable reaction policy in under 3 minutes*[note], plus a self-supervised pipeline that corrects in real time.

When the world drifts — friction, payload, sensor offset, wear — the named-variable calibration loop identifies which physical parameter changed and re-distills locally. No demo re-collection. No full retrain.

[note] Depends on task complexity: ~25 s for the Cube scorer, ~48 s for the TwoRoom scorer, ~168 s for PushT including reaction-policy distillation. All measured on a single Apple M1 Max + MLX. See the Verified Numbers table.

The Problem

Every custom robot deployment costs two weeks of engineering.

  1. Demonstration tax

    Diffusion Policy needs hundreds of teleoperated demos per task. VLA pipelines need millions. Every fixture or SKU change restarts collection. Weeks per change.

  2. Manual tuning

    When friction, payload, or sensor offset shifts, an operator opens the controller, eyeballs the trajectory, adjusts gains. Hours of expensive labor per drift event.

  3. Black-box drift

    End-to-end learned policies cannot tell you what changed. "Concept drift" is the diagnosis. Re-collect demos, re-train, redeploy. The cycle never gets cheaper.

Where do those two weeks actually go?

Typical allocation across the dominant industry approaches (Diffusion Policy, VLA, traditional PLC tuning):

Phase What gets built Equipment / tooling
Day 1–3 Spec definition + teleoperation demonstration collection (typically hundreds of demos per task) Teleop rig (ALOHA / Mobile ALOHA / Meta Quest leader-follower arms) · physical robot · 1–2 operators
Day 3–7 Data cleaning, labelling, simulator modelling, failure-case re-recording Engineer workstation · cloud storage · simulator (MuJoCo / Isaac Gym)
Day 7–12 Policy training (Diffusion Policy / VLA / RL), hyperparameter sweeps, evaluation Cloud GPU (A100 / H100, ~$5–$50 per cycle) · ML engineer · training framework (PyTorch / JAX)
Day 12–14 Sim-to-real calibration, manual tuning, integration test, documentation Physical robot · operator · controller hardware (Siemens / Rockwell / proprietary PLC)

Ranges are typical across public reports — Diffusion Policy, ALOHA / Mobile ALOHA, Physical Intelligence π0. Actual timelines vary by task complexity from one week to several. For large foundation-style VLA policies, data collection alone can take weeks to months.

The industrial robot market is roughly $200B. Most of it is gated by this engineering bottleneck. Meadow attacks the bottleneck directly.

Approach

Built for the deployment surface, not the leaderboard.

Three design commitments — kept deliberately at the level of what rather than how. The specific architecture that delivers these outcomes is the part we would rather discuss directly with the right counterpart, not publish in advance.

  1. Inputs are typed and named

    The system consumes a structured, named description of the robot, the scene, and the goal — not raw video streams. The benefit is that every variable that drives behaviour has a name an engineer can point at. This is what makes the next two commitments possible.

  2. The deployable artefact is small and fast

    Final policies ship at 0.077–2.04 MB and run at 0.095–0.122 ms per decision on consumer Apple Silicon. This is non-negotiable: if it cannot fit on the customer's laptop and respond inside a real control loop, we do not call it deployed.

  3. Drift is named, not opaque

    When a deployed policy starts failing in the real world, the system localizes which physical parameter has moved away from the assumption — friction, payload, sensor offset, contact stiffness — and corrects that one quantity locally, not the whole policy. This is the capability most existing approaches do not yet provide; it is also the reason a customer can recalibrate without sending data to the cloud.

The outcomes — sub-minute training, sub-millisecond inference, named-variable calibration — are reproducible from the public artefacts and videos on this page. The specific pipeline that connects them is intentionally held back; if you would like to evaluate it more deeply, please reach out.

Wedge Market

Semiconductor equipment calibration. Highest pain, highest willingness to pay.

A single EUV lithography tool costs $200M+. One hour of unplanned downtime burns hundreds of thousands of dollars. Operators currently diagnose chamber drift by reading sensor logs, tweaking parameters, and re-running test wafers — typically four hours per drift event.

Fabs already track named physical parameters: chamber pressure, laser drift, alignment offset. Meadow's spec-driven calibration loop maps directly onto their existing data structures with no paradigm shift required.

Founder is in Taiwan, within an hour's reach of the major fabs and their tier-1 equipment suppliers — a physical access advantage that overseas competitors do not have for early customer discovery and pilots. We do not name specific buyers in this public document.

Quantified value: 60 seconds of laptop time can replace 4 hours of operator analysis — roughly two orders of magnitude in operator productivity per drift event. Critically, all calibration happens on the engineer's local laptop; no wafer or proprietary data leaves the fab perimeter — which removes a real procurement blocker for IT security review.

Beachhead: design partners in the wedge vertical. Expansion: industrial robot integrators, scenario testing for autonomy, surgical robotics — the same approach generalizes once the first vertical is anchored.

Engineers describe the robot and goal in a typed, named specification; the system produces a deployable, sub-millisecond reaction policy that runs on the customer's local hardware. Q3

What's special about the approach?

  • Physical-first inputs. The system starts from named physical state, not pixels — the same starting point biological intelligence uses (vestibular, proprioceptive, contact signals before vision).
  • Sub-minute training on a consumer laptop. Trains in under 60 seconds on Apple Silicon — no discrete GPU, no cloud. A field engineer can recalibrate on her own machine during a coffee break.
  • Named-variable calibration. When a deployed policy drifts, we identify which physical parameter has moved — not opaque concept drift — and correct that one quantity locally.
  • Reproducibility as a first-class feature. Every metric in this document is verifiable from the public artefacts on this page; that level of disclosure is rare in the field today (see Figure 5).
